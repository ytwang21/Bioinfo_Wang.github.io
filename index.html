About

I'm a PhD candidate at Tongji University, working under Prof. Chenfei Wang on the single-cell biology of aging. My degree is formally in Biology, but my day-to-day work sits in bioinformatics: turning single-cell transcriptomic data into biological signal, and biological signal into interpretable models.

My current project, TabulaStromal, integrates single-cell transcriptomes of stromal cells — fibroblasts, endothelial cells, and their neighbors — across tissues to build a stromal aging clock, and to trace the genes and pathways that drive stromal aging back to potential therapeutic targets. Earlier, I helped build TISCH2, a tumor microenvironment single-cell database published in Nucleic Acids Research.

I'm drawn to methods that stay legible: elastic net over black boxes when a coefficient can point back to a gene, SHAP when a model must explain itself. My longer-term interest is in what stromal aging and tumor immunology can tell each other — and I'm looking for a postdoctoral position to pursue that question.

Research

TabulaStromal — a single-cell aging clock for stromal cells

First author · 2023 – present

Integrates and annotates single-cell transcriptomes of stromal cells across datasets to build a robust aging clock. The project identifies molecular signatures of stromal aging and the genes and pathways behind it, aiming to surface therapeutic targets for aging-related disease. Manuscript in preparation.

TISCH2 — Tumor Immune Single-cell Hub

Co-first author (2nd) · 2021 – 2023

Curated and manually annotated 190 human single-cell datasets of the tumor immune microenvironment, and built and maintained the database's website — handling both the data pipeline and the front and back end of the platform, plus ongoing user support. Published in Nucleic Acids Research.
